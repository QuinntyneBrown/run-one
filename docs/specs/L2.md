# L2 - Detailed Requirements

This document is rewritten for Telo Ride 1 and drops what Telo Ride 1 leaves out (see `L1.md`).
A requirement kept from Telo Ride keeps its ID, so the IDs have gaps; new requirements start at
L2-167, and a removed ID is never used again. Riding in Ibiza (L2-173 to L2-177) is new to
Telo Ride 1 and takes new IDs, though Telo Ride's courses were its starting point. The shape of
a ride (L2-179 to L2-182: the Ibiza Flyover, the countdown, the finish banner, and easing off
and rolling on) was added after every screen was built. Intensity and the sound of a ride in
Ibiza (L2-183 to L2-186, ADR 0050) were added after that, and built by journey 40.

Each L2 traces to one L1 in `L1.md`. Every acceptance test names the L2s it covers in a header
comment:

```typescript
// Acceptance Test
// Traces to: L2-011, L2-013
// Description: Pair an Echelon EX-5s from Connect Bike and land on The Class
```

The mocks (`docs/mocks/`) are the source of truth for layout and copy; quoted strings are
verbatim from them. Every mock draws Intervals to the Beat and, where the ride is in Ibiza, the
Ibiza Sunset Strip course; the few criteria no mock draws yet are listed under *Mock gaps*. The
criteria reuse the mocks' cast:

- **The rider:** one person on their own bike at home, at one of two stages:
  - **new rider:** has never paired a bike; no rides.
  - **returning rider:** 6 rides of the class, best output 342 kJ, last output 318 kJ.
- **The bike:** an Echelon EX-5s, identifier `EX5S-0bf669f1`, strong signal.
- **The class:** Intervals to the Beat (L2-080).
- **The course:** Ibiza Sunset Strip (L2-174).
- **Mid-ride moment:** 5:30 of 20:00, in the second recovery with 0:30 left; 233 W against a 231 W
  target, 91 rpm aiming 90, resistance 13 of 32.
- **Finished rides:** a new best of 351 kJ at 293 W average, its 17 efforts between 340 and
  427 W with the ninth the strongest; and a ride short of the best at 318 kJ, 265 W average, its
  efforts between 299 and 376 W.
- **The finish:** 20:00 of 20:00, the cool-down just ended; 187 W at 83 rpm, resistance 11 of 32.
  Rolling on four seconds later: 82 W at 70 rpm, resistance 4 of 32.

The cast's numbers are examples, not rules: an output, a best or a percentage is whatever the
rules below compute.

Terms are used in one sense only:

- **Resistance:** the level the bike reports.
- **Target resistance:** the level the app asks for.
- **Plan:** the timed segments the class follows. A **segment** has a kind (warm-up, work,
  recover, cool-down), a duration, a level and a cadence aim.
- **Effort:** one of the plan's 17 work segments.
- **Ride:** one run of the class. A ride is **finished** when its clock reaches the plan's
  length.
- **Output:** the work the rider did in a ride, in kJ.
- **Record:** the rider's ride count, best output and last output, kept on the device.
- **Course:** Ibiza Sunset Strip, the one road the rider can ride the class on in place of its
  video. **In Ibiza** means a ride drawn on the course; **with the class video** means the other.
- **Music:** the one background track, *Beautiful Boom Bap* (L2-176).
- **Flyover:** the course shown from above before a ride in Ibiza, on the screen Ibiza Flyover
  (L2-179).
- **Countdown:** the three seconds before the ride clock starts (L2-180).
- **Finish banner:** the banner that shows the ride's output when the ride is finished (L2-181).
- **Roll-out:** the seconds between the finish banner and Class Done, while the target
  resistance eases off (L2-182).
- **Blocked:** the browser cannot reach a bike, because it has no Web Bluetooth (L2-008) or the
  page is not secure (L2-009).
- **Diagnostic recording** (the **recording**): the timed, ordered list of what crossed the
  Bluetooth link and what the app made of it, kept only while it is switched on (L2-178). An
  **entry** is one line of it. The **person diagnosing** is whoever switched it on; a **note** is
  what they type of the bike's own display; a **screen capture** is one still picture of the
  app's tab; the **recording mark** is the "Recording" button every screen shows while it is on.

The screens are at `/connect` (Connect Bike), `/` (The Class), `/ride` (Ibiza Flyover, then Class
Ride) and `/done` (Class Done). Ibiza Flyover has no address of its own: it is what `/ride` shows
before a ride in Ibiza starts (ADR 0049).

Breakpoints are named as in the design system: **phone** < 640 px, **sm** ≥ 640 px,
**md** ≥ 768 px, **lg** ≥ 1024 px, **xl** ≥ 1280 px.

## L2-007: Supported bikes list
**Traces to:** L1-002

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Connect Bike, when it opens in any state, then a "Works with" section lists, in order: "Echelon EX-5s", "Echelon EX-3", "Echelon EX-8s" and "Echelon Connect Sport", each "Full control", and "Other FTMS smart bikes", "Beta".
2. Given an Echelon bike, when it is connected, then the app reads its telemetry and sets its resistance (full control, L2-013).
3. Given a bike that advertises FTMS (service `0x1826`) and is not an Echelon, when it is connected, then the app reads Indoor Bike Data and, if the bike grants control, sets its resistance (L2-014).
4. Given a viewport at sm or wider, when the list shows, then it is two columns; on a phone it is one.

## L2-008: Browser support
**Traces to:** L1-002

Web Bluetooth exists only in Chromium browsers (Chrome and Edge on Windows, macOS, Linux,
ChromeOS and Android).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a browser without `navigator.bluetooth` on a secure page, when Connect Bike opens, then the device card is replaced by an alert (`role="alert"`). It reads "This browser can't reach the bike" and "Open Telo Ride 1 in Chrome or Edge on a laptop or Android tablet. Safari and Firefox don't support Web Bluetooth yet.", with "Try again".
2. Given such a browser, when The Class opens, then an alert (`role="alert"`) under the header reads "Open Telo Ride 1 in Chrome or Edge." with "Bluetooth to the bike only works there — you can look around, but not ride." and **Copy link**. The class, its video and the rider's record stay readable.
3. Given such a browser, when the rider presses **Copy link**, then the page address is copied to the clipboard and the button shows its copied state.
4. Given a supported browser on a secure page, when any screen opens, then no unsupported-browser message is shown.

## L2-009: Secure context
**Traces to:** L1-002

Browsers allow Web Bluetooth only on HTTPS pages (and `localhost`). On a plain `http` page a
Chromium browser has no `navigator.bluetooth` either, so an insecure page is told to go secure,
never to change browser.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a page served over plain `http` other than `localhost`, when The Class opens, then an alert (`role="alert"`) under the header reads "Open the secure version of this page." with "Bluetooth is blocked on plain http, so the bike can’t connect." and **Open over HTTPS**. The link goes to the same path on `https://`.
2. Given a production deployment, when a request arrives over `http`, then it is redirected to `https` and responses carry `Strict-Transport-Security`.
3. Given a secure context, when any screen opens, then no HTTPS notice is shown.
4. Given a page served over plain `http` other than `localhost`, when Connect Bike opens, then the device card is replaced by an alert (`role="alert"`). It reads "Open the secure version of this page" and "Bluetooth is blocked on plain http, so the bike can’t connect.", with **Open over HTTPS**, which goes to the same path on `https://`.

## L2-010: Blocked rides
**Traces to:** L1-002

When the browser is blocked, the app says why and does not start a ride.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a blocked browser, when The Class opens, then the header reads "No bike · Connect", and the start button reads "Connect your bike", is disabled, and is followed by "The class needs the bike to set resistance.".
2. Given a blocked browser, when the `/ride` address is opened, then The Class opens instead and nothing is requested from Bluetooth.
3. Given a blocked browser, when the rider uses The Class or the page-not-found screen, then the video, the plan chart and the record work normally.

## L2-011: Connect Bike
**Traces to:** L1-003

Connect Bike (`/connect`) is where the bike is paired. A new rider starts here, and any rider
reaches it from The Class's header.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Connect Bike, when it opens, then it shows the wordmark, Full screen, the bike photo, "Connect your bike" and "Pedal once to wake it up. Telo Ride 1 reads your power and sets resistance for every hill and interval.", and no navigation.
2. Given the rider starts a search, when no bike has been picked yet, then the card shows a spinner with "Looking for bikes nearby…" (`role="status"`) and a disabled "Searching…" button.
3. Given Connect Bike at rest, when no bike has been picked, then the card names the offered model, "Echelon EX-5s", with no identifier, and the button reads "Pair Echelon EX-5s". Given the rider has picked a bike, when the card shows it, then it gives its name ("Echelon EX-5s") and identifier ("EX5S-0bf669f1") as text; a bike with no identifier shows its name alone. No signal strength is shown (open question 17).
4. Given the rider has picked a bike, when pairing runs, then the button is disabled with `aria-busy="true"`, a spinner and "Pairing…". Pairing starts with the pick; the rider does not press the button a second time.
5. Given pairing succeeds, when the bike is connected, then the card shows a check (`aria-label="Paired"`) and a disabled "Paired" button. About half a second later, The Class opens with the bike connected.
6. Given a viewport at lg or wider, when Connect Bike shows, then it is two columns with the photo on the left. Below lg, the photo follows the content at up to 28 rem, square.

## L2-012: Choosing the bike
**Traces to:** L1-003

A web page cannot scan in the background. The rider picks the bike once in the browser's own
chooser, which needs a tap or click.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Connect Bike, when the rider presses the search or pair action, then `navigator.bluetooth.requestDevice` is called with filters for the Echelon service (`0bf669f1-45f2-11e7-9598-0800200c9a66`) and FTMS (`0x1826`), and no other service is requested.
2. Given no user gesture, when the app would open the chooser, then it does not; nothing is requested from Bluetooth until the rider acts.
3. Given the rider cancels the chooser, when it closes, then Connect Bike returns to its default state with no error.
4. Given the chooser cannot open (a policy blocks it), when the request fails, then an alert (`role="alert"`) replaces the device card. It reads "Bluetooth is blocked for this page" and "Allow Bluetooth for this site in your browser’s settings, then try again." (any other failure reads "The bike list didn’t open" and "Something went wrong opening your browser’s list of bikes. Try again."), with "Try again", which opens the chooser again.

## L2-013: Echelon connection and initialisation
**Traces to:** L1-003

Bytes and UUIDs are in `docs/protocol/echelon-ble-frames.md`.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an Echelon bike is chosen, when the app connects, then it opens GATT, gets service `0bf669f1-…`, and subscribes to the notify characteristic `0bf669f4-…` before writing anything.
2. Given the subscription is in place, when initialisation runs, then it writes `F0 A1 00 91`, `F0 A3 00 93` and `F0 B0 01 01 A2`, in that order. Each goes to `0bf669f2-…` with response, at least 300 ms after the previous one.
3. Given any write to the bike, when another is pending, then the new one waits. Writes go one at a time in the order requested.
4. Given initialisation completes, when the first notification arrives, then the bike state is `connected`. Its name is the model's name when the device name is an Echelon's (`ECH-EX5S-141844` is "Echelon EX-5s", identified as `EX5S-141844`), otherwise the device name, or the device's identifier if it has none.

## L2-014: FTMS connection and control
**Traces to:** L1-003

Other smart bikes are supported in beta through the Bluetooth SIG Fitness Machine Service.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an FTMS bike is chosen, when the app connects, then it subscribes to Indoor Bike Data (`0x2AD2`) and to Fitness Machine Control Point (`0x2AD9`) indications, and reads Supported Resistance Level Range (`0x2AD6`).
2. Given the control point is subscribed, when the app needs to set resistance, then it first writes Request Control (op code `0x00`). Only on a Success response (`0x80 0x00 0x01`) does it send Set Target Resistance Level (op code `0x04`).
3. Given the bike refuses control or has no control point, when a ride runs, then telemetry, the clock and the video still work, no resistance command is sent, the resistance controls are disabled, and a notice under the bike panel on Class Ride says “The class can’t set this bike’s resistance. Set it by hand.” A bike that does not answer Request Control within 3 seconds has refused. Once a bike has refused, nothing more is written to it for the rest of the ride, after a reconnect included.
4. Given the bike's supported range, when the app maps levels 1 to 32 onto it, then level 1 is the minimum, level 32 the maximum, and levels in between are spaced linearly and rounded to the bike's increment.

## L2-015: The remembered bike
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a bike was paired in this browser, when Telo Ride 1 opens, then it opens on The Class without Connect Bike (L2-167). Where `navigator.bluetooth.getDevices()` exists, it reconnects to that bike without the chooser as soon as the bike advertises.
2. Given the remembered bike does not advertise, or `getDevices()` is not available, when The Class shows, then the bike is shown as not connected and the start button reads "Connect your bike" (L2-084 AC6).
3. Given the rider pairs a different bike on Connect Bike, when pairing succeeds, then that bike replaces the remembered one.
4. Given the remembered bike, when it is kept, then only its name and the browser's identifier for it are stored, on the device.

---

## L2-016: Keepalive
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an Echelon bike is connected and initialised, when 5 seconds pass, then between 4 and 6 keepalive frames `F0 A0 01 01 92` have been written, each with response.
2. Given the tab is hidden, when 60 seconds pass, then keepalives keep going at about one a second, timed from the clock rather than counted ticks (L2-133).
3. Given the bike disconnects, when it is gone, then keepalives stop until it reconnects.

---

## L2-017: Disconnect and recovery
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the bike is connected, when it disconnects (`gattserverdisconnected`) or any Web Bluetooth call fails, then the bike state becomes `disconnected`. The app retries the same bike every 3 seconds without the chooser until it connects or the rider leaves the ride.
2. Given the bike is switched off for an hour, when it is switched on and pedalled, then the app reconnects within one retry without a reload.
3. Given the rider presses **Reconnect**, when the bike is not connected, then the pending retry is cancelled and an attempt starts at once.
4. Given a reconnect succeeds during a ride, when the bike is back, then the current target resistance is sent again before anything else.
5. Given a Web Bluetooth error, when it is recorded, then its name and message go to the console as text (L2-115), and the rider sees only the app's own message.

## L2-018: Bluetooth off
**Traces to:** L1-003

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given `navigator.bluetooth.getAvailability()` resolves `false`, when Connect Bike opens, then an alert (`role="alert"`) replaces the device card and reads "Bluetooth is off" and "Turn on Bluetooth on this computer, then try again.", with "Try again".
2. Given Bluetooth is off, when the alert is shown, then it never says that no bike was found.
3. Given the rider presses "Try again", when Bluetooth is now on, then Connect Bike returns to its default state.

## L2-019: Bike status
**Traces to:** L1-003

The Class's header carries the bike status as a link to Connect Bike.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a connected bike, when The Class shows, then the header link reads the bike's name, "connected" and "· Change" ("Echelon EX-5s connected · Change").
2. Given no connected bike, when The Class shows, then the link reads "No bike" and "· Connect", with a different icon from the connected one, so colour is not the only difference.
3. Given the link, when the rider follows it, then Connect Bike opens.
4. Given a phone, when the link shows, then "· Change" or "· Connect" is hidden from sight but still read by a screen reader, and the link is at least 44 px tall.
5. Given the bike connects or disconnects while The Class shows, when the state changes, then the link and the start button (L2-084) follow within one second.

## L2-020: Echelon telemetry decoding
**Traces to:** L1-004

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a notification `F0 D2 01 0C CF`, when it is decoded, then resistance is 12.
2. Given a notification `F0 D1 09 00 05 00 00 00 00 00 50 00 1F`, when it is decoded, then cadence is 80 rpm.
3. Given a frame shorter than 4 bytes, with byte 0 other than `F0`, of an unknown type, or a `D1` shorter than 13 bytes, when it arrives, then it is ignored without an error.
4. Given a `D0` frame, when it arrives, then it is ignored without an error.
5. Given no `D1` frame for 5 seconds while connected, when the readouts show, then cadence and power show "—" rather than their last values.

---

## L2-021: FTMS telemetry decoding
**Traces to:** L1-004

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an Indoor Bike Data notification, when it is decoded, then its flags decide which fields are present. Cadence is uint16 in 0.5 rpm and power sint16 in W; every other field is skipped by its length.
2. Given the bike reports power, when power is shown, then it is the reported value, not an estimate.
3. Given a field the flags say is absent, when the readouts show, then that metric falls back to its estimate (L2-023) or "—".
4. Given a notification shorter than its flags require, when it arrives, then it is ignored without an error.

---

## L2-023: Power and output
**Traces to:** L1-004

The Echelon reports no power, so the app estimates it.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an Echelon with cadence 95 and resistance 18, when power is estimated, then it is max(0, round(95 × (0.55 + 18 × 0.155))) = 317 W.
2. Given cadence or resistance is unknown, when power is estimated, then it is unknown and shown as "—".
3. Given a segment with cadence aim 72 and level 6, when target power is computed, then it is 107 W by the same formula; with aim 90 and level 13 it is 231 W, and with aim 110 and level 22 it is 436 W.
4. Given each running second, when output is updated, then the second's power ÷ 1000 is added to the ride's output in kJ; a second with unknown power adds nothing.
5. Given a paused ride, when time passes, then output does not grow.

## L2-024: Simulated bike
**Traces to:** L1-004

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a development build opened with `?simulate`, when the rider connects, then a simulated Echelon EX-5s connects without the chooser and sends `D1` and `D2` frames once a second.
2. Given the simulated bike, when a set-resistance command is written, then its next `D2` reports the new level.
3. Given a production build, when `?simulate` is present, then it is ignored.
4. Given every feature, when it is exercised end to end, then it can run against the simulated bike.

## L2-025: Set resistance command
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given an Echelon and a target resistance of 12, when it is sent, then the bytes `F0 B1 01 0C AE` are written with response. For 32, the bytes are `F0 B1 01 20 C2`.
2. Given a target outside 1 to 32, when it is requested, then it is clamped to that range before it is sent.
3. Given an FTMS bike with control, when a target is sent, then it goes as Set Target Resistance Level, mapped by L2-014 AC4.

---

## L2-026: Target resistance and resistance
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a target resistance was sent, when the bike reports a different resistance, then the readout shows the bike's resistance ("13" and "of 32", `aria-label="Resistance 13 of 32"`), and the target stays the requested level.
2. Given the bike reports a new resistance, when the readout updates, then it shows the new value within one second.
3. Given no resistance has been reported, when the readout shows, then it reads "—".

---

## L2-027: Plan-driven resistance
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a class ride starts, when its clock starts, then the first segment's level is sent as the target resistance.
2. Given a running ride, when a segment starts, then its level is sent as the target resistance within 500 ms of the segment boundary.
3. Given a paused ride, when time passes, then no segment changes and no plan command is sent.
4. Given the bike reconnects mid-segment, when it is back, then the current segment's level is sent again.

---

## L2-028: Manual resistance
**Traces to:** L1-005

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a class ride with a connected bike, when the rider presses "Raise resistance" or "Lower resistance", then the target resistance moves by one level, clamped to 1 to 32, and is sent.
2. Given the rider changes the level by hand during a segment, when the next segment starts, then the plan's level, at the ride's intensity (L2-183), applies again.
3. Given the bike is lost, when the resistance controls show, then both buttons are disabled.
4. Given a paused ride with a connected bike, when the resistance controls show, then they stay enabled.

## L2-077: Best output
**Traces to:** L1-013

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a finished ride whose output, in whole kJ, is higher than the record's best, when Class Done opens, then the `h1` reads "New best output", the output is in gold, and a fact reads "Previous best" with the earlier best ("342 kJ").
2. Given a finished ride whose output is equal to or lower than the best, when Class Done opens, then the `h1` reads "Your best is still {best} kJ" ("Your best is still 342 kJ"), the output is not gold, and a fact reads "Best" with the best.
3. Given the rider's first finished ride, when Class Done opens, then the `h1` reads "Class done. That’s your number to beat.", the output is not gold, and there is no best fact.
4. Given a new best, when it is shown, then the words "New best output" carry it, never the gold alone.

## L2-084: The Class
**Traces to:** L1-014

The Class is the home screen, at `/`.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given The Class, when it opens, then it shows:
   - the header with the wordmark, the bike status (L2-019) and Full screen;
   - the video player with its controls, parked at the sync offset (0:03), or a still of the course when the rider has chosen Ibiza (L2-173 AC3);
   - "GCN Training · Intervals", the title as the `h1`, and the summary;

## L2-178: Diagnostic recording
**Traces to:** L1-003

When a bike will not connect, will not report pedalling or will not take a target resistance, the
person diagnosing must be able to answer, for any moment of a ride: what bytes did the bike send,
what did the decoder make of them, what did the screen show, and what was the bike really doing.
The recording is a diagnostic aid, not a feature for the rider. It adds no screen and no menu
(L2-167 AC4), and every rider gets the app with it off.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the app opened at an address without `diagnose` in its query, when it is used, then nothing is recorded, no screen shows the recording mark or any other control of the recording, and none of the recording's code is fetched, by the page or by the app's service worker. Every screen is what it is without this requirement, and the initial bundle is no larger (L2-136 AC1).
2. Given the app opened at an address with `diagnose` in its query (`/?diagnose`), in a production or a development build, when it loads, then the recording's code is fetched, the recording starts before the first Bluetooth call, and every screen shows the recording mark: a button that reads "Recording". The query is read once, when the app opens, and in one place. The recording lasts until the tab is closed or reloaded; nothing about it is stored, so the app opened again without `diagnose` records nothing.
3. Given the recording is on, when the bike sends bytes (a notification, an indication, or the answer to a read), then one entry holds them before they are decoded: the time, the direction, the characteristic and every byte as hex. A frame the decoder drops is recorded the same way (L2-129 AC4).
4. Given a notification the app reads telemetry from, when it is recorded, then the same entry holds what the app's decoder made of those bytes: which decoder the app uses for this bike (`EchelonFrameDecoder` or `IndoorBikeDataDecoder`, chosen as the readouts choose it), the frame it recognised (`D1`, `D2`, or Indoor Bike Data with its flags), and the cadence, resistance and power it produced; or "dropped" with the reason (L2-020 AC3, AC4; L2-021 AC4). Given `F0 D2 01 0C CF` the entry reads resistance 12, and given `F0 D1 09 00 05 00 00 00 00 00 50 00 1F` cadence 80. An Echelon frame's entry also says whether its checksum holds, which the app itself does not check. An FTMS bike's other bytes are read on their entries too: the resistance range, and the control point's answer.
5. Given the recording is on, when the app writes to the bike (an initialisation command, a keepalive, a resistance command, an FTMS control point command), then one entry holds the write's number, the characteristic and every byte as hex when the write is made (its turn in the write queue has come, so a wait behind another write is not counted), and a second entry holds the same number and its outcome when it settles: the time the bike took to acknowledge it, or the error it failed with. A resistance command's entry also holds the target resistance the app meant to request.
6. Given the recording is on, when the link changes, then an entry is added for each of: the chooser opening with its filters, and its outcome (the bike picked, closed without one, or failed); the device's name and identifier; each service the bike offers with its characteristics; the link chosen (Echelon or FTMS); each `ble` state; a drop; each reconnect attempt and its outcome; and, for a remembered bike, that it was found without the chooser and that the app waited for it to advertise. Listing a service's characteristics is the one call the recording adds to the link; a bike that refuses it is recorded and connected as it would have been.
7. Given the recording is on, when the app logs an error (L2-115), then the line the console gets is added to the recording, redacted as the console's is.
8. Given Class Ride with the recording on, when a readout the rider sees changes (power, cadence, resistance, or the target resistance), then one entry holds all four as the screen shows them, so bytes, decoded values and what the rider saw line up by time. The screen's "—" is written `"-"`, since the file is ASCII (AC17).
9. Given the recording, when it grows, then it holds at most 20,000 entries and 3,000,000 bytes of text; past either, the oldest entries go first. A saved recording and the recording's dialog say how many entries were dropped. Screen captures are held apart, at most 8; the oldest goes when a ninth is taken, and the recording says so. Nothing else of the recording grows with the ride's length (L2-138 AC2).
10. Given every entry, when it is made, then its time is milliseconds since the recording started, read from the monotonic clock (`performance.now()`), and entries are in the order they happened.
11. Given the recording mark, when the person diagnosing presses it, then a dialog titled "Diagnostic recording" opens over the screen with the number of entries, a field for a note, **Add note**, **Capture screen**, what a saved recording holds (AC16), **Save files** and **Close**. The ride carries on behind it. It is a modal dialog: focus stays in it, Escape closes it, and focus returns to the recording mark.
12. Given the dialog, when the person types what the bike's own display reads (for example "bike shows 82 rpm, resistance 14") and presses **Add note**, then one entry holds the note as text with its time, the field empties and the dialog says "Note added". A note is at most 200 characters; **Add note** is off while the field is empty.
13. Given the dialog in a browser with the Screen Capture API, when the person presses **Capture screen**, then the dialog closes, the browser asks its own permission to share the tab, and one still picture of the tab is kept with the recording, with an entry that holds its number, its file name and its size in pixels at the time it was taken. The browser's sharing ends as soon as the picture is taken. The dialog opens again and says "Screen capture 1 kept".
14. Given the browser's permission is refused or the capture fails, when it ends, then no picture is kept, an entry says so, and the dialog opens again and says "The browser didn’t share the screen, so no capture was kept. The ride carries on." Given a browser without the Screen Capture API, then **Capture screen** is off and the dialog says "This browser can’t capture the screen. Notes and the recording still work." Either way the ride, the bike and the recording carry on.
15. Given the dialog, when the person presses **Save files**, then the browser saves the recording as one text file, `telo-ride-1-recording-<UTC time>.log`, and each screen capture as a PNG named as its entry names it, and the dialog says how many files were saved. Given the browser cannot save them, then it says "The files couldn’t be saved. Try again." and the recording is kept. No request leaves the device for a recording, a note, a capture or a save (L2-131, L2-132), and the Content Security Policy is not changed.
16. Given the dialog, when it is open, then it says, before anything is saved: "The recording holds your bike’s name and identifier and every Bluetooth message. A screen capture holds whatever is on the screen. Nothing is sent anywhere: the files are saved only when you press Save files."
17. Given a saved recording, when it is read, then it is plain text, one entry a line: lines that begin `#` say what the file is, its format's version, when the recording started, the address and the browser, the limits and the number of entries dropped; every other line is the time, the entry's kind (`RX` bytes from the bike, `TX` a write, `ACK` a write's outcome, `EVT` a link event, `ERR` a logged error, `SCR` the readouts shown, `NOTE`, `CAP` a screen capture) and its detail. Bytes are upper-case hex separated by spaces, as `docs/protocol/` writes them. Text from the bike or the person (a device name, an error, a note) is quoted and escaped, never markup (L2-129 AC1), and the file is ASCII.
18. Given the simulated bike (L2-024) or an FTMS bike (L2-014), when it is used with the recording on, then what it sends and what is written to it are recorded as an Echelon's are.
19. Given a ride with the recording on, when it runs, then the readouts stay within L2-134 and L2-136 AC3, and a ride left open for 3 hours stays within L2-138 AC1.
20. Given the recording on, when the tab is closed, or a capture ends, then every stream and its tracks, timer, listener and object URL the recording took is released.

36. **Where the recording mark sits (L2-178 AC2).** It is fixed at the foot of the screen, at the

    L2-178 AC1 forbids when it is off and which was not done when it is on. The mark is in every
    screen capture. Decide whether the screens should make room, and whether a capture should
    leave the mark out.
37. **The bike's kind is read from its name, the link from its services.** `BikeTelemetry` decodes
    by `BikeInfo.kind`, which the bike's name decides, while the link is chosen by the services the
    bike offers. A bike whose name and services disagree would be decoded by the wrong decoder.
    The recording shows both (L2-178 AC4, AC6), which is how such a bike would be found; fixing it
    is not this requirement's.

    taken the mark reads "Capturing…". A dropped screen capture is said in the recording only,
    not in the dialog. All are built so and drawn so in the mocks; none was decided by a person.

39. **The design's newest Class Ride is not followed (L2-087, L2-180 to L2-182).** In the thread
    *Ride 1 Class Flow* the prototype's Class Ride was redrawn "like Mario Kart": the video or the
    course edge to edge with the readouts over it, a resistance readout with no stepper, each
    effort called out as it arrives ("Drop 4", "Final drop!", "Recover"), and efforts renamed
    "drops". These requirements take from it only what was asked for: the flyover, the countdown,
    the finish banner, easing off and rolling on, each drawn in Class Ride as it is built (the
    video's or the course's box above the bike panel). The edge-to-edge layout would replace
    L2-087 and L2-089 and every Class Ride mock; the call-outs are a new requirement; "drop" is a
    second word for "effort", which `AGENTS.md` does not allow. ADR 0049 records the decision
    as it stands: the mocks in `docs/mocks/` draw Class Ride as built, and they are the source of
    truth. Taking the edge-to-edge layout or the call-outs later is a change to the mocks first.
40. **The design's class and course are not the product's (L2-179).** The same thread drew a
    class called "20 Minute HIIT" (eight 30-second efforts, ending on the last effort, with no
    cool-down) and a course that is "one 3.2 km loop" of Sant Antoni bay with a marina at the
    start and the finish. The class is Intervals to the Beat (L2-080), which ends on a 2:30
    cool-down, and the course is 8.4 km from a grid to a finish line (L2-174). The flyover's five
    captions were rewritten here from the course as it was built (`docs/class-worlds.md`); they
    are a proposal. The design's stills of the course are its targets, not the course as built:
    the mock uses one for the view from above until the app can supply its own.
41. **Music before the ride (L2-176 AC1).** The music starts on "Go!". The flyover and the
    countdown are silent. A course intro with music under it is what the design's model has;
    decide whether the track should start with the flyover.
42. **The timings are the prototype's (L2-179 to L2-182).** 9 seconds of flight, 0.9 seconds on
    the grid, a countdown of 3.7 seconds, 3.2 seconds of banner, 5.2 seconds of roll-out, a level
    every 110 ms down to level 4: all are the numbers the prototype plays at, and none was tried
    on a bike. Seven Echelon writes in under a second are within what the write queue takes, but
    whether the bike's motor follows that fast is not known. Level 4 is the prototype's; an FTMS
    bike gets it mapped (L2-014 AC4).
43. **A new best on the finish banner (L2-181 AC8).** The design's banner shows the output in the
    accent colour and says nothing of the best. Class Done sets a new best apart in gold
    (L2-078). Decide whether the banner should say it first.
44. **The flyover every time (L2-179).** It plays before every ride in Ibiza and can be skipped.
    Decide whether a returning rider should see it at all, or only the first time.
45. **The flyover's camera and the course's budgets (L2-179 AC10, L2-177).** The course was built
    to be seen from behind the rider. From above, more of it is in view at once: whether a frame
    stays within 120,000 triangles and 140 draw calls there, and what the map looks like from
    above where nothing was modelled, is not known until it is drawn. The cheap form, if it does
    not hold, is the camera low over the road rather than high above the bay.
    *Answered for the camera as built (behind 40 m, above 55 m, looking 60 m ahead): the flyover's
    frames at the heaviest step stay within both budgets (`class-ride-ibiza-budgets.spec.ts`).*
46. **No way out of the roll-out (L2-181 AC5, L2-182).** For 8.4 seconds after the finish the
    rider can do nothing but watch: the design shows no button. Class Done follows on its own and
    nothing is lost, so no timing criterion of WCAG 2.2 is broken, but decide whether a press
    anywhere should open Class Done at once.
47. **The default video's name (L2-185, L2-186).** `xfgZRgPkoME` is called "Default video"
    because its title could not be read from YouTube when the requirement was written. Name it
    once someone has watched it, and decide whether its length covers the ride's 20:00; a video
    that ends early leaves the rest of the ride without sound.
48. **YouTube audio and YouTube's policies (L2-186 AC2, ADR 0050).** A hidden player separates
    the audio from the picture, which YouTube's API Services Developer Policies forbid. The owner
    accepted the risk. If YouTube objects, YouTube audio is taken out.
49. **Intensity and the best output (L2-183, L2-077).** A ride at 8/10 can set a best that a ride
    at 5/10 cannot reach. The record does not say at what intensity a best was set; decide
    whether it should.

## Carried-over code

Telo Ride's features that these requirements leave out are removed from the code and its tests
(ADRs 0036 to 0043 and 0047), the backend among them (slice 28.8). The ADRs before 0036 still
describe Telo Ride and are kept as history.

Riding in Ibiza keeps part of what was carried over: the Ibiza Sunset Strip course
(`tools/blender/courses/ibiza.json`, `courses/ibiza.glb`), the course renderer in the `world`
library and the Blender build for that one course (ADR 0034). Telo Ride's other seven courses
were removed by slice 30.1 (ADR 0042), and the rest of the `world` library with what served it
by slice 37.1 (ADR 0047): World loading and the world downloader, the class video inset in the
course, the panoramas, Telo Ride's world scenes and its map renderer, the other riders of a
race (bots, ghosts and pacers), and all 192 tracks of the worlds' music. *Beautiful Boom Bap*
(L2-176) was added by slice 37.5. A ride in Ibiza is designed in
`docs/detailed-designs/class-ride/ride-the-class/`.

The loading screen ADR 0047 removed does not come back with Ibiza Flyover: the flyover is the
course itself seen from above, it never holds the ride for a download, and the rider can skip it
(L2-179, ADR 0049).

`SentCommand` (`api`, `bike/sent-command.ts`) was carried over with Telo Ride's packet log and is
kept: `ResistanceController` holds the last one for Class Ride's stepper. The diagnostic
recording (L2-178) does not use it; it records every write at the write queue, with the target
resistance beside a resistance command. Telo Ride's Diagnostics dialog, its packet log and its
connection history stay removed (ADR 0040); ADR 0048 says what the recording brings back.
