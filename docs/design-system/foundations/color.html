Foundation

Color

Ink, panel and raised for surfaces; bone for text; signal orange for the rider and the next step; gold only for something earned. Dark first, with a light theme, and every pairing checked.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stable

Overview

Telo Ride 1 is dark first: the design draws ink surfaces, bone text and one accent, the Telo signal orange (the design calls it “volt”). A light theme of paper and deeper accents was added by this system; the design never draws it. Colour is used sparingly, and each colour has one job:

  • Signal orange (--color-volt): the primary button, the chosen option, efforts in the plan, “On target”, “Go!”.
  • Blue (--color-rest): recoveries in the plan.
  • Gold (--color-gold-fg): a new best output on Class Done, and nothing else.
  • Red (--color-heart-fg) and amber (--color-warn-fg): the bike lost or Bluetooth off; something short that the ride survives.

Components read semantic tokens by role, never a palette value or a hex.

On pictures

The video and the course are pictures the system does not control, so anything laid over them carries its own ground. Words on a picture are --color-on-photo (bone in both themes) on a scrim that is also the same in both themes: --color-scrim-photo behind the countdown’s numeral and the loading line, --color-scrim-strong behind small text (the countdown’s lights and line, the roll-out card, “Paused”).

The finish banner brings its own band: the inverse surface, with the output’s numerals in --finish-banner-accent (the deep signal on the light band of the dark theme, the signal on the dark band of the light theme).

Usage

  • Read tokens by role: --color-fg, --color-panel, --color-volt-fg. Never a hex, never a palette token in a component.
  • Colour is never the only carrier of a state: “On target” is a word, the chosen option has a filled mark, the strongest effort says so in text.
  • One accent per view does the pointing. Gold is not decoration.
  • To add a brand theme, redefine the surface, text and accent tokens under a new [data-theme] and run the contrast check; see Theming.

Accessibility

Both themes pass WCAG 2.2 AA for every listed pair (1.4.3, 1.4.11). prefers-contrast: more strengthens borders and subtle text; forced-colors maps the focus ring and borders to system colours.
