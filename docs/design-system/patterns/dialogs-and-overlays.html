Pattern

Dialogs and overlays

Almost nothing is laid over a screen. What is, lies inside the video’s or the course’s box, or is the one alert a ride cannot do without.

Overview

The rider’s app opens no dialogs. Its overlays are of two kinds: the ride’s moments, which lie inside the box and ask nothing (countdown, “Paused”, finish banner, roll-out), and the lost-bike alert, which covers Class Ride under its header. The one real dialog is the diagnostic recording’s, outside the rider’s app.

When to use what

NeedUse
Mark a moment of the rideAn overlay inside the box; no controls, no focus, pointer-events: none.
Stop the ride until the bike is backThe lost-bike alert.
Edit something behind a buttonA CDK Dialog or another screen; never an inline form. No screen needs one today.
The diagnostic recordingThe platform’s <dialog>, opened with showModal(): a sheet at the foot below 640px, a centred card 448px wide from 640px.
Confirm leaving a rideNothing. Leave leaves.

Structure

The lost-bike alert over Class Ridepages/class-ride/disconnected
The diagnostic recording’s dialog over Class Ridedialogs/diagnostic-recording/default

States

The lost-bike alert has one state per ride mode (its body names the video or the ride). The diagnostic dialog’s states are on its own page.

Responsive behaviour

  • In-box overlays scale with the box.
  • The lost-bike alert’s card is centred, at most 384px wide.
  • The diagnostic dialog is a sheet at the foot of the window below 640px.

Accessibility

  • In-box overlays speak through one role="status" each and take no focus.
  • The lost-bike alert is role="alert"; the header stays above it and usable.
  • The diagnostic dialog is modal: focus is trapped by the platform, Esc closes it, focus returns to the recording mark.
  • Only one overlay at a time.

Content

  • An overlay says one thing: “Paused”, “Finish!”, “Lost the bike”.
  • An alert’s action names what it does: “Reconnect”.
